A Postcard from Gaurav IIT Kharagpur → Data Scientist

Hey,

I'm writing this from my desk in a tech company, where I get paid to teach machines how to predict things. Seven years ago, I had no idea this job existed. Ten years ago, I was a student like you — just trying to figure out the next step, and honestly, still trying to figure things out today.

I chose engineering because I liked math and I liked figuring out how things worked. That was it. I didn't have a master plan. I didn't know what "data science" meant. I didn't know what AI was. I just kept saying yes to things that pulled me — a project here, a course there, a question that wouldn't leave my head.

Engineering didn't give me a job. It gave me a toolkit. A way of thinking. Once you learn to break a hard problem into smaller ones, you can apply it to anything — building software, running a company, solving problems in your own village.

I've worked at Zomato, at OLA, and now at a global logistics company. I've published research in top conferences like ICASSP. I help build Saarthi alongside my work. None of these were on any list I was shown in Class 10.

Here's what I want you to know: you don't need to know your destination at 15. I didn't. Most of the people I admire didn't either. You just need to pick a direction, walk honestly, and stay curious. Engineering is one such direction — wide, forgiving, and full of doors you can't even see yet.

If you love asking "how does this work?" — you'll be fine here.

— Gaurav · IIT Kharagpur, 2021 · Data Scientist
If this road feels right

Here's how to walk it — from Class 10 to where I am today

A practical map for anyone just finishing Class 10. No fluff. Same voice as the letter above.

Now → Class 11–12

Pick PCM. Math is the engine.

Take Physics, Chemistry, Math. Math is the most important — engineering is mostly applied math. If your school offers it, add Computer Science as the optional subject. It'll save you a year of catch-up later.

Don't stress about the ‘right school’. Both government and CBSE schools produce IIT toppers. Effort beats brand.

Class 12 → Entrance exams

These open the doors

  • JEE Main — almost every engineering college worth attending uses this score
  • JEE Advanced — for IITs (you have to clear JEE Main first to sit for it)
  • BITSAT — BITS Pilani, Goa, Hyderabad — genuinely great alternatives to IITs
  • State CETs — your state's own exam (MHT-CET, KCET, KEAP…) — often lower cutoffs and reserved seats
  • CUET — for central universities like Delhi University, JMI, BHU
Picking a college

Don't obsess over IIT.

IITs are great. They're not the only path. NITs, IIITs, BITS, and top state colleges all produce people earning ₹50 LPA. I know data scientists from ‘tier-3’ colleges who out-earn many IIT classmates.

The college matters less than what you do once you're inside it.

Picking a branch

Pick what excites you, not the ‘highest package’

  • CSE / IT — broadest entry to software, data and AI roles
  • ECE — chips and signals; also pivots well to software
  • Mechanical / Civil / Electrical — core engineering. Slower start but very stable
  • Chemical / Biotech — pharma, FMCG, process industries
  • AI / Data Science degrees — newer; some colleges now offer these directly
During college

What separates good engineers from great ones

  • Build projects you can show on GitHub
  • Take 2+ internships before graduating
  • Join clubs — coding, robotics, the entrepreneurship cell
  • Take electives outside your branch
  • Learn one programming language deeply (Python is a safe bet)
After engineering

Six honest options

  • Software / data / AI engineer at product companies — Razorpay, Flipkart, Google, Microsoft
  • Higher studies — MS abroad, MBA in India or abroad, PhD
  • PSU / government — ISRO, ONGC, BARC, RBI
  • Civil services — UPSC. Many engineers crack it.
  • Startup — join an early-stage company and learn how things are really built
  • Founder — start your own (like Aniket did)
You're 15. You don't need to know your final career today. Pick PCM if engineering excites you, work honestly, stay curious — and the doors will open. — Gaurav
