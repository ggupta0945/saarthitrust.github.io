A Postcard from Anju KV → IIT Kharagpur (Economics, 2021) → UPSC CSE 2024, AIR 60 → IAS Officer

Hi,

Before I tell you my story, I want you to know one thing.

I failed three times before I succeeded.

When I cleared UPSC Civil Services 2024 with All India Rank 60, people called me a topper. Articles got written. But what most of them didn't say loudly enough — I gave the exam four times. Three of those four times, my name was not on the list.

I'm telling you this because somewhere in a small town in India right now, a student is reading about toppers and thinking — "they are different from me. They cracked it in one go. I will never be like them." I want you to know: I was you.

I'm not from a big city. My father had a transferable job, so I grew up moving between Kendriya Vidyalayas — new city every few years, making friends, leaving them, starting over. After Class 12, I prepared for JEE and got into IIT Kharagpur for a 5-year Economics program. At that point my dream was simple: finish IIT, get a good corporate job, settle down. I didn't even know what UPSC was.

Then 2020 happened. COVID lockdown. I sat in my IIT room and watched the news — migrant workers walking hundreds of kilometres home, barefoot, hungry, carrying children on their shoulders. Families crying because they had no food. And one thought wouldn't leave me: "If I were in administrative power right now, I could actually do something." By the end of lockdown, I had decided. Civil servant. Not for salary or prestige — for the chance to be useful when my country needed people to be useful.

When I started UPSC preparation, I knew nothing. I didn't know what prelims looked like. I didn't know what an "optional subject" was. I didn't know which books to read. So I went to YouTube. Free videos. Topper interviews. I built my own strategy. Not someone else's. Not a coaching's. Mine.

Attempt 1. I failed.
Attempt 2. I failed.
Attempt 3. I failed.
Attempt 4. AIR 60.

Three years of waking up, studying, writing tests, waiting for results, refreshing that PDF and not finding my name. People around me getting promotions, marriages, holidays — and I was still at the same desk I sat at three years before.

But every failure was teaching me something. The girl who gave the 4th attempt was a completely different person from the girl who gave the 1st — sharper, calmer, more prepared. The failures weren't blocking the success. They were building it.

The fourth time, I gave the exam with everything I had. Got married a few days before the result was announced. And then the list came out. AIR 60. Five years from "what is UPSC?" to IAS officer.

Walk toward your dream long enough — and it walks back to meet you. Three failures didn't change mine. They made me ready for it.
— Anju · IAS Officer · UPSC 2024, AIR 60
If this road feels right

Here's the honest map — for anyone thinking about civil services

A practical step-by-step for someone in Class 10 or 12 considering UPSC down the line.

Now → Class 11–12

Pick the stream that holds your attention

UPSC has no "right" feeder stream. Doctors, engineers, lawyers, B.A. graduates — all crack it. Pick what you'll actually read for hours without checking the clock.

Read newspapers from now. The Hindu, Indian Express, or BBC News India online — 20 minutes a day. UPSC's whole worldview comes from understanding India and the world; build that muscle early.

Undergrad

Any degree from a college that lets you read widely

  • If you crack JEE / NEET — go. IIT, NIT, BITS, AIIMS all welcome UPSC dreamers.
  • If commerce / arts — Delhi University, JNU, SRCC, Christ, Ashoka, Krea, top state universities
  • If it doesn't have to be famous — a tier-2 college with a strong library and free evenings is actually perfect UPSC prep ground
  • What matters — pick a degree that lets you read history, economics, politics, current affairs on the side
First exposure

Figure out if this actually calls you

Most people who crack UPSC say there was a moment — a news event, a personal experience, a book — that made them think "this is the work I want to do." For me it was the migrant crisis of 2020.

If no such moment has happened, that's fine — don't force it. UPSC takes 2–4 years of full-time prep. Don't start unless something inside you keeps coming back to it.

The exam structure

Prelims → Mains → Interview

  • Prelims (May–June) — multiple-choice. Filters out ~98% of candidates. Pure knowledge.
  • Mains (Sept–Oct) — nine 3-hour written papers. Tests reasoning, writing, ethics, your optional subject.
  • Interview / Personality Test (Jan–Apr) — 30–40 minutes with the UPSC board. Tests judgment.
  • Cycle — one full cycle takes ~12 months. Most aspirants commit 2–3 cycles.
Preparation reality

Coaching is one tool. Discipline is the real one.

You don't need expensive coaching. I cleared with YouTube + free notes + my own discipline. Many toppers do.

  • Free resources — Vision IAS notes (free PDFs), Drishti IAS YouTube, ForumIAS, Insights on India, Unacademy free lectures, NCERTs
  • Coaching helps if — you struggle with structure on your own, or your optional needs an expert
  • Self-study works if — you can sit at a desk for 8 hours a day without supervision
  • What matters most — showing up at the desk on the days you don't feel like it. That's the entire game.
The failure mindset

Most who crack it failed first. Plan for it.

Around 70% of selected candidates take 2+ attempts. Many take 3 or 4. Failing the first time is the norm — not the exception.

Have a Plan B that respects you. A job in a corporate or PSU you can fall back on. Family support. Or savings that buy you 2–3 years. UPSC is a marathon, and the people who finish are the ones who can afford to keep running.

You don't need to be a topper. You don't need to know your dream at 15. You just have to be someone who refuses to stop trying. That's the whole secret. — Anju
